A columnar keyboard, also sold as a column staggered keyboard, arranges its keys in straight vertical columns and moves each column up or down so that it matches the finger above it. Most columnar boards are split, small, and built around thumb clusters: groups of keys pressed with the thumbs. The best-known names are ZSA's Moonlander and Voyager, the open-source Corne and Sofle, and the MoErgo Glove80. The wider comparison with row-staggered and ortholinear boards is in staggered vs ortholinear vs columnar keyboards.

What is a column staggered keyboard?

A column staggered keyboard has straight columns of keys, each shifted vertically to fit its finger, where a classic keyboard offsets its rows sideways. The xcmkb documentation puts it this way: keys align vertically within each column "but with a stagger to match the natural lengths of your fingers." Deskthority describes columns shifted at different offsets to better suit the different fingers, and gives no values.

Makers argue from finger motion. ZSA writes that a finger extended on a columnar board does not go sideways, so reaching above and below the home row is easier. Kinesis says straight columns match natural finger motion better than staggered rows. Wikipedia names a cost: removing the row stagger increases the distance from the index finger's neutral position to some of its secondary keys.

The row stagger that columns replace is a typewriter inheritance, covered in the comparison guide. Ortholinear boards drop it too but keep every column at the same height, which makes a Planck ortholinear and a Corne columnar. Sources blur the line: Switch and Click calls the Moonlander and the ErgoDox EZ split ortholinear boards, and Dygma calls the Defy a split columnar design "similar to ortholinear keyboards".

What do column stagger and thumb clusters change for your fingers?

Most letters stay with the finger that already presses them. The index finger keeps two columns, and the pinky keeps the outer ones. The changes sit where the staggered board was ambiguous or overloaded.

The bottom row changes first. On a staggered board it sits half a key right of the home row; on a column board B sits below G and N below H, so each stays with its index finger. Next is the number row: boards with three letter rows, such as the Corne with its 3x6 grid, have none, and numbers go on a layer. Last are Enter, Space, Backspace and the modifiers. Kinesis puts Enter, Space, Backspace and Delete in the thumb clusters of the Advantage2 and says this shifts work from the little fingers to the thumbs.

The finger chart in the finger placement guide gives the thumbs only the space bar, so thumb clusters add a new finger job to learn. Boards differ widely: MoErgo says all six thumb keys of the Glove80 are reachable without moving the palm, ZSA gives the Moonlander an articulating thumb cluster whose module tilts up and down, and Dygma lists a 16-key thumb cluster on the Defy. In the petitdoigt keyboard database, 72 of the 73 columnar boards have a thumb cluster.

Colemak-DH has an angle mod that adds symmetry on a standard staggered board. The Mod-DH page recommends the original mapping for all other keyboard types, ergonomic and ortholinear boards included. The Colemak guide has the layout.

How many columnar keyboards are there, and what kinds?

The petitdoigt keyboard database lists 73 column staggered boards out of 989. Of those 73, 65 are split into two halves, 7 are angled one-piece boards (Keyboardio Atreus and the X-Bows line, Dygma Sonsei), and 1, the Kinesis Advantage2, is one piece. By kind, 31 are prebuilt, 33 are kits and 9 are open designs sold as PCBs. Twenty-three are low-profile builds, and 12 have curved key wells, such as the Kinesis Advantage family, Glove80 and Bastard Keyboards' Charybdis.

Firmware counts, with some boards listing two: QMK on 42, ZMK on 33, Vial on 14 and VIA on 12. Tenting is built in on 9 boards and an accessory on 48. The split keyboard guide covers what splitting and tenting change. The keyboard finder picks the matching boards out of the database from a description such as "column staggered wireless keyboard with tenting".

Which open-source columnar keyboards exist?

The open designs are the Corne, Sofle, Lily58, Ferris and Ferris Sweep, and the Kyria. All are split with thumb keys, and builders can buy a PCB, a kit or a pre-soldered set from vendors.

The Sweet Setup reviewer of the Planck EZ guessed the Moonlander would have a shorter transition time than the Planck, which he found demanding. That is one person's expectation. How do you practice on a columnar keyboard?

Write the finger assignments down and start slowly. Each letter keeps its finger, the thumbs may take keys the pinkies used to press, and layers add key combinations with no counterpart on a staggered board. petitdoigt shows a finger-coded keyboard on screen and has drills for single fingers, including the anchors drill for returns to the home row. It records which keys you press and cannot see your thumbs, so it does not cover a thumb cluster's layout. The touch typing guide lays out a practice plan, and the keyboard sizes guide places small boards among the other sizes.
