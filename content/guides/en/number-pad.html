A number keypad is the block of keys at the right of a full-size keyboard that types digits in calculator order, with 7 8 9 on top. Data-entry tests call it the ten-key, and typists work it by touch with the right hand, three fingers on a home row of 4 5 6. Many compact keyboards drop it, so it is also sold as a separate pad. In the petitdoigt keyboard database, 290 of the 989 boards come in a size that keeps it, and 9 are number pads sold on their own.

What is a number keypad?

The standard PC number keypad has 17 keys: the digits 0 to 9, the four arithmetic keys / * - +, a decimal point, Num Lock and its own Enter. Its digits follow calculators and cash registers, with 7 8 9 two rows above 1 2 3. A telephone keypad runs the other way, 1 2 3 on top, so a phone habit sends the fingers to the wrong row. The 5 key may carry a raised bar, which Wikipedia says is there to help enter numbers by touch.

Separate number pads often add keys. FILCO's Majestouch TenKeyPad 2 Professional added Esc, Tab, Backspace and a 00 key, and the REALFORCE RT1 has 23 keys. Both the FILCO and the Leopold FC220TP keep a Num Lock of their own, independent from the main keyboard.

How do you type on the number pad without looking?

Rest three fingers of your right hand on the middle row: index on 4, middle on 5, ring on 6. Find the bump on 5 with your middle finger first. Each finger then owns the column above and below its home key, so 7 and 1 go to the index finger, 8 and 2 to the middle finger, 9 and 3 to the ring finger. Every digit from 1 to 9 is then at most one key from a home key.

Teaching materials agree on those nine keys and split on the rest. Carol Finch's guide for It Still Works and Peter Kolomiets's for JobCannon use five fingers, with the thumb on 0 and the pinky on Enter; Finch also gives + and - to the pinky. A CAERT keyboarding unit published by the Illinois State Board of Education teaches three fingers only and sends the index finger down to 0 and the ring finger across to Enter.

The five-finger method keeps the three home fingers on their columns and hands the large keys at the edges to the thumb and pinky. The three-finger method leaves the thumb and pinky out and stretches the ring finger up to the operator keys and down to Enter. Pick one and keep it, the same rule that holds for the letter keys in the keyboard finger chart.

The thumb and the eyes

Kolomiets advises that the thumb tap 0 and leave: a thumb resting on the key drags the whole hand low. The CAERT unit asks for curved fingers that tap the keys "as if they are on fire," with the middle finger staying on 5 and the eyes on the screen or the paper, never on the keypad. That last habit is the one the guide to typing without looking builds for the letter keys.

What is a good 10-key speed in keystrokes per hour?

Ten-key speed is counted in keystrokes per hour (KPH), and about 8,000 KPH is the figure most often given as average. The test publisher Mettl writes that many data-entry roles treat 8,000 KPH as the average standard and that 10,000 KPH sets a candidate apart. Criteria Corp's Ten Key Test has 20 entries, takes under five minutes and reports a speed score in KPH next to an accuracy score, the number of correct entries out of 20.

These figures are conventions. Kolomiets, writing for JobCannon, which runs its own ten-key test, calls the 8,000 average "a rule of thumb, not a measured norm," and its bands apply to an adjusted KPH, raw speed multiplied by accuracy:

If Enter counts, that is 8 keystrokes an entry and 12,000 KPH; if it does not, 7 keystrokes and 10,500 KPH. The typing is identical, and the two scores sit a whole band apart. Words per minute, the measure for text, is explained in the guide to typing speed.

Number pad or number row: which should you use?

Use the number pad for long runs of figures and the number row for the odd digit inside text. On the pad, all ten digits sit under one hand around a home key you can find by touch. On the number row they spread across both hands, two rows above the home row, and each finger reaches up and back for every digit; the finger chart shows three competing ways to finger that row. Corsair recommends a full-size board to anyone who works with spreadsheets, saying "the numpad makes data entry a breeze."

The layout can tip the choice. On French AZERTY, the number row types accented letters when unshifted, and its digits need Shift; the AZERTY vs QWERTY comparison goes through that row key by key. Laptops usually leave the number pad out, and a separate one can be added over USB.

What is a 10-keyless keyboard?

A 10-keyless keyboard is a tenkeyless (TKL) board: a full-size keyboard with the number pad cut off. Corsair describes it as "same as the Full-size, without the Numpad," keeping the arrows and the function row. The Deskthority wiki counts 87 keys on an ANSI TKL, against 104 on a full-size board. Smaller sizes drop more, and the 96% size squeezes the pad back in beside the arrows.

Can you get a keyboard with the number pad on the left?

Yes. Left-handed keyboards put the pad there, and detachable or separate pads can go on either side. The Deskthority wiki defines a left-handed keyboard as one with the order of its key clusters reversed, which puts the number pad on the left and clears room for the mouse on the right, useful to right-handed people too. Its maker list includes Strong Man, whose design is still sold as the DSI left-handed keyboard. Wikipedia adds that a number pad can stand in for the W A S D keys in games, an option for left-handed players who hold the mouse in the left hand.

Modular boards let you choose the side. MOUNTAIN says the number pad of the Everest Max attaches on either the right or the left, and the Everest 60 is "ready for the Everest 60 Numpad on the left or right side." The X-Bows Knight Plus comes with a detachable 21-key magnetic numpad. Any of the separate pads above can sit to the left of a keyboard as well.

Split ergonomic boards handle the pad in two ways. The Logitech ERGO K860 builds it into a curved, split keyframe, and the Microsoft Sculpt Ergonomic Keyboard ships it as a separate wireless unit. The guides to split keyboards and ergonomic keyboards cover those shapes.

How do you practice 10-key typing?

Practice the home row of the pad first and add speed last:

  1. Find the bump on 5 with your right middle finger, and rest your index and ring fingers on 4 and 6.
  2. Type columns: 7 4 1 with the index finger, then the middle and ring columns, returning to the home key after each tap.
  3. Add 0 and Enter with the fingers your method assigns, and use them in every entry.
  4. Type figures from a sheet of paper or a second window, keeping your eyes on the source and off the keypad.
  5. Time yourself on a ten-key test only once the fingering is automatic, and note whether it counts Enter.
