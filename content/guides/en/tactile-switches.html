Tactile switches give a small bump in the middle of the stroke, at the point where the key registers, and make no click. They sit between linear switches, which have no bump, and clicky switches, which add an audible click to the bump. The three together make up the feels of keyboard switches.

What is a tactile switch?

A tactile switch is a keyboard switch whose force rises to a bump and falls again as the key travels, so your finger feels the actuation point. Cherry's tactile colors are Clear, Brown and Tactile Grey. Clicky switches add a click on top of the bump.

A spec sheet shows the bump as a third force. Cherry's MX2A Brown lists 45 cN to actuate, 55 cN for the bump peak and 80 cN at the bottom, over a 4 mm stroke with actuation at 2 mm. The MX2A Red, a linear switch with the same 45 cN actuation force, has no bump figure. The guide to linear vs tactile vs clicky sets the three feels side by side.

Where does the bump sit, and how strong is it?

The bump sits around the actuation point, and its strength is the gap between the bump peak and the actuation force. Makers state the peak as a force, so the table gives that gap in force units (cN for Cherry, gf for the rest).

A light bump is 5 to 10 units above the actuation force (Kailh's Box Brown, Cherry's MX2A Brown and MX Clear), and a heavy one is 20 or more (Cherry's MX Falcon and MX2A Purple). The absolute force is a separate choice: the Petal pairs a 15 unit bump with a 30 cN actuation force, so the whole press stays light, while the Burnt Orange needs 60 gf before its 10 unit bump. Actuation comes 2 mm into a 4 mm stroke on the Brown and 1.7 mm into 3.5 mm on the Falcon. Glorious's original Panda actuates later, at 2.5 mm of a 3.3 mm stroke, with 49 gf actuation and 60 gf at the bottom.

A few makers do not publish the bump, so the table leaves out Gateron's tactile lines. Gateron's G Pro 3.0 Brown lists 55 gf and the Oil King Yellow Heavy Tactile 60 gf, both without a peak figure. The petitdoigt switch database holds 241 tactile switches from 42 makers and 53 silent tactiles, and 75 of those 294 list a bump force.

What are the best known tactile switches?

Well-known tactile switches include the Cherry and clone Browns, the Pandas and the Bobas.

  • Brown style. Cherry's MX2A Brown, Gateron's G Pro 3.0 Brown (55 gf, 2 mm / 4 mm) and Kailh's Box Brown share the light bump and a 2 mm pre-travel. Cherry's MX Clear moves the bump peak to 65 cN, and its Tactile Grey lists 70 cN actuation and an 80 cN bump, a heavy option Deskthority notes is usually reserved for space bars.
  • Holy Panda. ThereminGoat describes the original Holy Panda as a Halo True or Halo Clear stem in an Invyr Panda housing. Drop's later Holy Panda X was designed with Quakemz, Invyr and Mech27 and produced by Gateron; ThereminGoat's review credits it with consistency and smoothness and a well-balanced sound, and notes spring ping and a scratchy bump. Drop's own product page no longer exists, and neither Holy Panda has force figures from the maker. RTINGS reviewed the Drop and Invyr Holy Panda.
  • Glorious Panda. A separate switch from Glorious, with a 49 gf actuation force, a 60 gf bottom-out and 3.3 mm total travel on its original page. ThereminGoat reviewed it in 2020 as a high-tactility alternative to Drop's Holy Pandas, sold unlubed and ready for modding.
  • Gazzew Boba U4T. A long-pole tactile. Pantheon Keys sells it in 62 gf and 68 gf bottom-out weights, with light factory lube. RTINGS has a review of the 62 gf version. Gazzew has no official spec page, so those figures come from the retailer.
  • Kailh Canary. A custom-color tactile on the Box stem, listed at 42 gf actuation and 60 gf bottom-out.

Are there quiet tactile switches?

Yes. Silent tactile switches damp the bump's noise and the return stroke. Cherry's MX2A Honey lists 45 cN actuation, a 55 cN bump and 110 cN at the bottom, with actuation at 1.9 mm of a 3.7 mm stroke. Gateron's Oil King Purple Tactile Silent lists 55 gf and 1.5 mm of 3.8 mm, and Keychron's Silent Banana lists 55 gf. Matias's Quiet Click is an Alps-style tactile switch damped on the downstroke and the upstroke, and it makes no click. Gazzew's U4Tx is a semi-silent tactile with pads on the stem that quiet the upstroke. The quiet switches guide covers every feel.

Is a tactile switch better for typing or gaming?

No source has shown tactile switches to be faster or more accurate than linear ones, for typing or gaming. Tactile switches give a felt actuation point, linear switches a uniform press, and the right one is the one you prefer after trying both.
