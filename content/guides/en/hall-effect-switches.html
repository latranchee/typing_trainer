A Hall effect switch measures where the key is. A small magnet travels with the stem, and a sensor on the circuit board reads how strong the magnetic field is at that moment. The keyboard therefore sees the whole stroke instead of a single on or off event, which makes an adjustable actuation point and rapid trigger possible. The overview of keyboard switches covers the other sensing methods.

How do Hall effect switches work?

The key press moves a magnet closer to a Hall sensor, and the sensor outputs a signal that follows the magnetic field. A Hall element is a thin strip of metal carrying a constant current; electrodes on its sides pick up a voltage proportional to the magnetic field component perpendicular to that current. A linear Hall sensor passes that value on continuously, so the keyboard's firmware can turn it into a key position.

A conventional mechanical switch closes a metal contact at one fixed point in the stroke. A Hall switch has no contact to close. The firmware compares the sensor reading with a threshold you choose, and the key registers when the reading crosses it. On the Wooting 60HE v2, Hall effect sensors on the PCBA detect the magnet inside each Lekker switch.

The idea is older than gaming keyboards. Honeywell engineers developed Hall-effect keyboard switches in the late 1960s, when they were costly and used mostly in aerospace and military systems.

Because the sensor lives on the board, the switch itself holds a magnet and nothing else electronic. The petitdoigt switch database lists 129 magnetic switches, from Gateron, Kailh, TTC, Wooting, SteelSeries, Corsair and many more, and 119 of them are linear. A handful are tactile or clicky, and a few are silent.

What is adjustable actuation?

Adjustable actuation means you pick the depth at which each key fires. On a contact switch that depth is built into the metal; on a Hall switch it is a number in software.

Wooting's page for the 60HE v2 says its software, Wootility, sets each key's actuation point from 0.1 mm to 4.0 mm in 0.1 mm steps. Other makers publish their own ranges:

What is rapid trigger?

Rapid trigger makes a key follow the direction of movement. As soon as the key moves up by a small distance, it releases; as soon as it moves down again, it presses, wherever it is in the stroke. A normal switch has to rise past its fixed reset point before it can fire again.

Wooting says its Rapid Trigger resets and activates keys the instant you move, lets press and release sensitivity be tuned separately, and includes a Continuous Rapid Trigger mode, without giving distance figures. Razer's page for its analog optical switch describes the same behaviour: keys reset at the slightest lift of a finger, which Razer presents as useful in shooters and rhythm games.

In the petitdoigt keyboard database, 185 boards list rapid trigger, and 206 of 989 records have adjustable actuation. The feature belongs to the firmware and the sensor together, and it appears on optical analog boards too, such as the Razer Huntsman V3 Pro (see optical switches). To list the boards with both, describe one to the keyboard finder, for example "hall effect 65% keyboard with rapid trigger", and it filters the database.

What is analog input?

Analog input means the keyboard reports how far a key is pressed, not only whether it is down. Wooting says its boards track the whole movement of each keypress. Games that read this value can map the press depth to speed or steering, so a half press moves a character or a car partway.

Only games and software written for it use the value. Tom's Hardware's review of the Wooting Two HE made that point directly: highly configurable and fun to tinker with, but analog input needs supported games. For ordinary typing the extra data goes unused, and the key behaves like any other once its actuation point is set.

What is the difference between Hall effect and TMR switches?

TMR switches use the same magnet-in-the-stem design and read it with a different sensor. Tunnel magnetoresistance is an effect in a magnetic tunnel junction, two ferromagnets separated by a thin insulator, whose electrical resistance depends on the alignment of the magnetic layers. An outside magnetic field changes that alignment, so the resistance tells the sensor about the field. According to Wikipedia, these junctions are replacing Hall sensors in many position-sensing uses.

Keyboards sold as TMR offer the same feature set. The Logitech G512 X has sockets that take its linear TMR analog switches or ordinary 3-pin and 5-pin mechanical switches. Akko, Epomaker, Cherry Xtrfy and Monsgeek also sell TMR boards. Cherry Xtrfy's K5 Pro TMR Compact earned 76/100 from PC Gamer as a comfortable 65% board with rich competitive features.

A third method, inductive sensing, appears on the Ducky One X. RTINGS wrote that its inductive switches set actuation as precisely as the best Hall effect boards, though they sit loosely in the PCB.

Which keyboards and switches use Hall effect sensing?

Every large gaming brand sells at least one.

  • Wooting makes the Lekker switch, and the 60HE v2 carries the Lekker Tikken Medium. The Tikken Medium is listed at 42 gf start and 54 gf end with 4.0 mm travel.
  • SteelSeries launched OmniPoint with the original Apex Pro in 2019. The Apex Pro Gen 3 uses OmniPoint 3.0, with Rapid Trigger, Rapid Tap and Protection Mode.
  • Corsair introduced MGX Hyperdrive with the K70 MAX. The K70 Pro TKL, MAKR PRO 75 and Vanguard PRO 96 use MGX switches.
  • Razer has a Hall effect line: the Huntsman V3 HE Magnetic and the hot-swappable Reclusa Mini 65.
  • Gateron sells magnetic switches that other brands put in their boards: the KS-20 line, Magnetic Jade Pro, which lists a 36 gf initial force, and dozens more variants in the petitdoigt switch database.
  • Keychron and Lemokey have dozens of "HE" boards, from the Q1 HE to the K2 HE.
  • Logitech G uses magnetic analog switches in the PRO X TKL Rapid and G515 Rapid TKL, and TMR in the G512 X.

Hot-swap sockets for magnets exist, and 113 boards in the petitdoigt keyboard database list them. They only take magnetic switches built for that board. Wooting says the 60HE v2 does not detect switches without the magnet. A stock hot-swappable keyboard with contact sockets cannot read a magnet at all, so the two switch families are not interchangeable.

Do Hall effect switches make typing faster?

No source measures typing speed on Hall effect boards. The published benefits concern response time, adjustable actuation and games that read analog input. The trainer offers finger-coded drills in the browser, with key sounds recorded from 13 real switches that you choose from a dropdown in its settings, and makes no claim about speed gains. Start with the touch typing guide and pick whichever switch lets you press keys without pressing extras.
