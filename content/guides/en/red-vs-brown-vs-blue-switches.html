Red, brown and blue are the three classic colours of the Cherry MX line, and each names a type of switch: red is linear, brown is tactile and blue is clicky. Other makers copied the scheme, some of them with different forces, and Cherry's 2023 MX2A revision kept the colours. The full set of switch kinds sits in keyboard switches, and the feel of each kind in linear vs tactile vs clicky.

What do the Cherry MX colours mean?

The colour is the stem, the part of the switch that carries the keycap. MX variants are generally referred to by stem colour, and Deskthority sorts them into three groups: linear, tactile and clicky. A linear switch has no bump, a tactile switch has a bump at the point where the key registers, and a clicky switch adds a click to that bump.

  • Red is linear. Deskthority lists it at 45 cN, with Speed Silver and Silent Red at the same force.
  • Brown is tactile, listed at 55 cN. Clear is also tactile, at 65 cN.
  • Blue is clicky, listed at 60 cN. Dark Blue and Soft Click Clear (both 50 cN) and White (80 cN) are clicky too.
  • Black is linear and heavier, listed at 60 cN.

Green and the two greys exist for space bars. The cN unit is Cherry's: one centinewton is close to one gram-force, which most other makers use.

Two of the Cherry pages disagree with themselves. The MX2A Brown page lists 45 cN in its property table and 55 cN as operating force in its highlights, and the MX Ergo Clear page lists 40 cN in the table and 55 cN in the highlights. The table above takes the property-table value for actuation and the highlight value for the Brown's bump, so read the Brown as close to 45 and 55 cN.

The three colours differ in what happens as the key passes its actuation point. Red goes through with no event. Brown gives a small bump that you feel. Blue gives the bump with a click that you hear, and Das Keyboard's loudness table puts the MX Blue above the Brown and the Silent Red. The clicky switches guide covers how the click is made.

The three colours predict the type reliably at Gateron, Kailh's classic line, Logitech and Outemu. Elsewhere they stop doing so. Razer runs a separate scheme in which green is the clicky switch. Gateron uses yellow for a 50 gf linear and green for an 80 gf clicky, and Kailh's Box line names its clicky switches White, Jade and Navy. Forces vary within a colour too: Gateron's Ink V2 Blue is listed at 75 gf, well above the 60 gf of its standard Blue, so check the data sheet for the force. Across the petitdoigt switch database, all 72 switches with Red in the name are linear or silent linear, and 46 of the 47 Browns are tactile or silent tactile. Blue is looser: 37 of the 64 Blues are clicky.

Gateron's KS-8 Red lists the same figures as Cherry's Red: 45 gf with 2 mm pre-travel and 4 mm total travel, against Cherry's 45 cN, 2 mm and 4 mm. The switch specs guide explains how to read these numbers and what the stated ones leave out.

What is MX2A, and did the colours change?

MX2A is the revision Cherry announced in August 2023. It covered MX Black, Brown, Red, Blue, Speed Silver and Silent Red. Cherry's press release says the update brought a new barrel-shaped spring, factory-applied lubricant on the socket dome and diamond-polished sliding surfaces in the housing, which reduce scratch and spring noise.

The names carried over. Cherry's pages now say MX2A Red, MX2A Brown and so on, and MX Red is another name for MX2A Red. The press release lists the full-size line as Red, Brown, Black, Speed Silver and Silent Red with RGB versions of each. MX Blue is the exception: it gets no lube and no barrel spring, so that it keeps its click.

Older MX1A parts still turn up. Cherry's pages for MX Clear and MX Green carry the classic MX1A part numbers, and telcontar.net lists the variants. Deskthority notes it was unclear whether MX2A would replace the older MX1A and MX3A parts, so a keyboard sold as Cherry MX Brown may have either generation inside. The product page of the exact model settles it.

Which should you pick?

The colour gives you the type. Red, or another linear, has no feedback between top and bottom. Brown is the tactile entry, with a bump that tells you where the key registers. Blue is the clicky one, and it is also the loudest of the three.
