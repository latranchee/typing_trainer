The IBM Model M keyboard is the desktop keyboard whose buckling spring switches define the "clicky" feel. A coil spring under each keycap collapses partway through the press, and the keyboard gives a distinct jolt and a loud sound at that moment. IBM built it from 1985, and Unicomp builds it today. The guide to linear, tactile and clicky switches places it among the other feels.

How does a buckling spring work?

A buckling spring switch lets a spring kink instead of compress. Four steps make it work: pressing the key compresses a coil spring that sits between the keycap and a pivoting hammer; partway down, the spring buckles, which means it kinks or collapses; the sudden loss of force pivots the hammer forward; the hammer strikes an electrical contact, which registers the keypress. On a Model M that contact is a membrane sheet, similar to the one in a dome-switch keyboard.

The patent for the mechanism is US 4,118,611, "Buckling spring torsional snap actuator", issued to IBM on 3 October 1978 with Richard Hunter Harris as inventor. The force graph in the patent shows a fast drop where the spring buckles. Because the drop comes as the hammer fires, feedback and actuation coincide.

Unicomp describes its version in the same terms: each key has a coil spring under the keycap and a hinged hammer that collapses when pressed. The guide to keyboard switches covers the other mechanisms.

What is the difference between the IBM Model F and Model M?

The Model F senses each spring through a capacitive circuit board, and the Model M uses a plastic membrane instead. The Model F has full n-key rollover and an actuation force of about 600 mN, lighter than the Model M, with a crisper feel and louder feedback. The Model M's changes cut manufacturing costs.

The Model F first appeared with the IBM System/23 Datamaster and is best known from the IBM PC of 1981, followed by the PC/AT in 1984. IBM did the principal design work on the Model M in 1983 and 1984, production began in 1985 and the first unit shipped with the 3161 terminal. The PC AT and PC XT shipped with it from 1986.

IBM spun its keyboard production into Lexmark on 27 March 1991, Lexmark's five-year purchase agreement ended on 27 March 1996, and Lexmark left the keyboard business in April 1996. Wikipedia estimates more than 10 million Model Ms shipped under IBM and Lexmark. IBM Japan moved in the late 1980s to a buckling spring design made by Brother Industries, first used in the 5576-003 keyboard.

Who makes the Model M today?

Unicomp does. The company says on its About page that it started in 1996 when it bought keyboard technology from Lexmark and IBM; Lexmark had made the Model M for IBM computers and terminals before that. Unicomp manufactures in Kentucky and lists a Lexington address. Unicomp kept using the original IBM machinery, and in 2020 it shipped a "New Model M" made with replaced tooling. Unicomp designed a 104-key version with Windows keys in 2000.

How loud and heavy is a buckling spring keyboard?

Unicomp describes the New Model M as a heavy, audible keyboard that gives tactile and auditory feedback on every keystroke. It states a peak force of 72 g, plus or minus 20 g, total travel of 3.8 mm and a rating of 25 million keystrokes. The board itself weighs 3 lb 11 oz (about 1.7 kg). The petitdoigt switch database records the same 72 g peak and 3.8 mm travel for the Unicomp buckling spring.

The sound varies by era. A 1986 Model M makes the characteristic buckling spring sound. A 1999 M13 is quieter, with less spring noise, and a 1984 Model F sounds sharper and more metallic because its spring plates contact the board directly. Forces come from different sources and measures: Wikipedia gives the Model F an actuation force of about 600 mN, and Unicomp gives a peak force for the New Model M. Check which board and which measure each figure describes before comparing them.

If noise is a problem where you work, the guide to quiet switches lists silent options, since buckling spring boards are loud. The guide to switch specs explains force curves and travel.

Is a Model M good for typing?

The Model M is a full-size board with the standard key positions, so touch typing works as on any keyboard. Its feel is a firm force with a distinct buckle and sound on every key press.
