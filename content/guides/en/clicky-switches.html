Clicky switches make an audible click. Each press reaches a point where a small part inside the switch snaps, and the key registers at about that moment. They are one of the three feels in keyboard switches; linear vs tactile vs clicky switches compares all three.

What are clicky switches?

A clicky switch is a tactile switch with a sound added. A tactile switch gives a bump you feel as the key passes its actuation point. A clicky switch gives the bump too, and a separate part of the switch produces an audible click at the same instant. Linear switches have neither: the key travels smoothly from top to bottom.

The click comes from the mechanism itself. The case and keycaps change its color and how far it carries.

How does the click work: click jacket, click collar or click bar?

Three mechanisms make the click.

  • Click jacket or collar (Cherry MX Blue and its clones). Deskthority describes a two-piece slider with a movable contact that fires a click collar downward when the key passes the actuation point; the slider pulls the collar back up on release. Wooting calls the same part a click jacket and says it slams against the bottom housing, with the release hitting the upper housing.
  • Click bar (Kailh Box White, Jade and Navy). Kailh's pages describe the White's click as coming from a click bar instead of a two-part stem, and the Jade and Navy as having a thick click bar.
  • Click leaf or torsion spring (some Gateron and Kailh switches). Gateron's Harmonic uses a click leaf, and Kailh's Box Summer a torsion-spring click.

Cherry's 2023 MX2A update added factory lube and a barrel-shaped spring to most of its MX line. MX2A Blue is the exception: Cherry says it keeps its click mechanism without that lube and spring.

Which switches are clicky, and how heavy are they?

Cherry publishes forces in cN and the others in gf; 1 cN is close to 1 gf. Force at the actuation point decides how hard you press, and the click or tactile force is the peak of the bump. The petitdoigt switch database holds 96 clicky switches from 26 makers, 24 each from Gateron and Kailh, and their median stated actuation force is 52 gf.

Which keyboards come with clicky switches?

Many boards offer a clicky option next to a linear and a tactile one, so the same model can be bought in all three kinds. Examples:

  • Logitech G512 ships in GX Blue Clicky, GX Brown Tactile or GX Red Linear. The G915 X line adds the low-profile GL Clicky.
  • Razer BlackWidow V4 comes with Razer Green (clicky) or Razer Yellow (silent linear); the Huntsman Mini is sold with a clicky or a linear optical switch.
  • Ducky One 3 offers Cherry MX Blue among its options, and Dygma Raise 2 offers Kailh Box White next to its linear and silent options.
  • Unicomp New Model M uses buckling springs, an older mechanism that clicks without a switch in the modern sense. PC Gamer calls its typing excellent, and Custom PC calls the board heavy and loud. The guide to the buckling spring covers it.

How loud are clicky switches, and what about the office?

Clicky switches are the loudest kind. Das Keyboard writes that they are designed to be loud, and Wooting traces the sound to the click part striking the housing on the way down and again on release. The rest of the keyboard shapes the noise: thin keycaps give a higher pitch and thicker ones a lower pitch, a hollow case works as an echo chamber, and a hard desk carries vibration.

Coworkers hear the click on every keystroke, including the release, and fast typing makes a continuous rattle. Typing on a friend's board or a switch tester in the same room before buying shows what the neighbors would hear. Where noise is a worry, the quiet switches guide lists the alternatives.

Das Keyboard and Wooting list changes that tone a clicky board down: lubing the switches and stabilizers, O-rings on the keycap stems, foam between the plate and the case, a desk mat and heavier keycaps. These soften the thud and the rattle around the click. The click stays, since the switch exists to make it.

Should you pick a clicky switch for typing?

The click confirms that a key registered, and some typists like that confirmation. The bump and the click also add force: the table shows peaks of 55 to 95 gf on the Box range alone, while a typical light linear needs 45 gf from top to bottom with no peak. The guide to the best switches for typing looks at what the sources say about force, travel and fatigue.
